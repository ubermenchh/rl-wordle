# How the games end: SFT, RL run 1 and RL run 2 (2026-10-17)

Source: the trace files of the 400-game held-out evals (`scripts/analyze_failures.py`). Same 400 tasks for the three models.
Run 1 and SFT: old Modal account, runs `eval-eval-rl-wordle-1011-131936-1011-201833` and `eval-eval-sft-wordle-1011-113953-1011-202720`.
Run 2: new account, `eval-eval-rl-wordle-1017-110828-1017-135110`.

| How the game ended | SFT | RL run 1 | RL run 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Won | 30 (7.5%) | 52 (13.0%) | 39 (9.8%) |
| Lost: legal replies, 6 turns used | 94 (23.5%) | 194 (48.5%) | 192 (48.0%) |
| Illegal: not a word | 147 (36.8%) | 90 (22.5%) | 84 (21.0%) |
| Illegal: wrong length | 95 (23.8%) | 56 (14.0%) | 64 (16.0%) |
| Illegal: repeat | 30 (7.5%) | 8 (2.0%) | 20 (5.0%) |
| Illegal: not letters | 4 (1.0%) | 0 | 1 (0.2%) |
| Wins in the games that did not end on an illegal reply | 30 of 124 (24%) | 52 of 246 (21%) | 39 of 231 (17%) |
| Legal replies that fit all feedback so far | 200 of 864 (23%) | 228 of 1208 (19%) | 216 of 1190 (18%) |

What it means:

- RL moved games from "illegal" to "lost". The illegal share fell by about 30 points, the lost share rose by about 25 points, and wins rose by 2 to 5 points.
- Among the games that stayed legal, the win rate did not rise (24%, 21%, 17%). So the gain in wins comes from more games surviving, not from better play.
- Only about one legal reply in five fits all the feedback, before and after RL. RL did not teach the model to use the feedback. The shares are not a clean per-reply rate: the RL models play more late turns, where a consistent word is harder to find.
- Almost all illegal replies are spelling errors: 87% of the `not_a_word` replies (73 of 84, run 2) are one edit from a legal word. Repeats are the smallest kind and RL cut them most.
- Run 1 almost removed illegal first replies (18, from 72 after SFT). Illegal replies at turns 2 to 5 fell less.
- Hypothesis, not tested: the reward ladder pays the step "legal" (0.1) and the step "consistent" (0.3) the same way in every state, and the first step is easy to learn. A run with more weight on consistency, or an SFT that teaches the reasoning line better, might move the 20%.
- Not measured: whether the reasoning line itself states the right constraints; other temperatures.
