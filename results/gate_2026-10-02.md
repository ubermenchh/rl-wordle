## Decision rule (TASKS.md task 4, plus the dense-signal column)

- `nondegen` (solved): at most 1% on every stratum 1-4 in every config. Bar is 20%. **Fails.**
- `nd_consist` (the deduction signal): at most 9%, and only few-shot at prior 1; 0-4% elsewhere. **Fails.**
- `nd_reward` is high for plain and few-shot (81-94%), but that is spelling variance (legal word vs non-word / no tag), not deduction. Constrained removes the spelling variance and `nd_reward` falls to 14-21%. A reward-std-only gate would have said "go" on a model with no deduction signal.
- Constrained decoding fixes validity and repeats by construction, but does not create a signal: `consistent` stays at 0%.

## The finding that explains it: worse than random on the feedback

Measured random-from-pool baseline on the same eval states (pool = ANSWERS minus guesses):

| prior | P(random pick is consistent) | mean consistent words in pool |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 9.3% | 83.7 |
| 2 | 1.1% | 9.8 |
| 3 | 0.3% | 2.4 |
| 4 | 0.2% | 1.5 |

At prior 1 the constrained model scores under 0.5% against 9.3% for a random pick (1,600 samples, so about 150 expected hits against fewer than 8 observed). Inspecting four prior-1 states (not a general claim, small sample): it copies the previous guess's letters, e.g. TRULY gives TRUNK / TRUTH / TRUCK, WHAND gives WHALE (10 of 16), IMSHI gives SHIRT (11 of 16), with 2.5-3.2 letters shared with the prior guess and 0 consistent samples in all four. It is anchoring on the displayed guess and ignoring the Grey list, which is the behaviour SFT on correct, constraint-consistent traces is meant to remove.
